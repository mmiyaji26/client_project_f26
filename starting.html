Starting Your Experience

Turn an Initial Idea into a Shared Plan

The starting phase establishes professional communication, productive first meetings, clear roles, and a project plan that all stakeholders understand. The goal is not to solve everything in the first meeting; it is to create the conditions for learning and collaboration.

Start with the Partnership

First impressions shape the trajectory of a partnership. Approach the client or community partner as a collaborator and learner. Listen for priorities, constraints, and definitions of success before proposing solutions.

  • Draft a clear, respectful introduction that names your team, course, and reason for reaching out.
  • Confirm the best contacts, communication channels, meeting times, and response expectations.
  • Ask what the partner already knows about the project, its history, and the people affected by the work.
  • Share what your team can contribute while being honest about your availability, skills, and limits.

Plan the First Meeting

Use a shared agenda so the kickoff meeting makes space for the partner’s knowledge and ends with clear next steps.

  1. Welcome everyone and confirm the purpose of the meeting.
  2. Invite the partner to describe the organization, community, project history, and current need.
  3. Discuss intended users, constraints, existing work, and what a useful outcome would look like.
  4. Introduce team members and clarify each person’s role in communication and decision-making.
  5. Review possible deliverables, milestones, risks, and questions that still need research.
  6. Confirm the next meeting, owners for follow-up tasks, and how decisions will be documented.

Create a Team and Project Agreement

Write down the decisions your team makes so expectations do not depend on memory. Revisit the agreement when project conditions change.

  • Roles: Who leads partner communication, meeting notes, research, project tracking, and deliverables?
  • Communication: Which channels will the team use, how quickly should members respond, and when will the team meet?
  • Decisions: How will the team make choices, handle disagreement, and include partner feedback?
  • Scope: What is included in the project, what is out of scope, and what would require a new agreement?
  • Access: What information, people, tools, or permissions are needed to begin responsibly?

Resources for Starting Well

  • Project Scoping Handout and Terms: Translate early conversations into realistic goals and boundaries.
  • Project Management Worksheet: Assign responsibilities and map work to a timeline.
  • “Check Yourself” Community Engagement Checklist: Prepare for respectful, reciprocal communication with partners and communities.
  • Contact the Engaged Learning Office: Ask about project expectations, partnership support, or available templates.
