During Your Experience

Navigating Your Active Project

Real-world projects rarely follow a straight line. Use this guide to manage changing requirements, maintain communication, gather feedback, and connect technical decisions to human needs.

Why Friction Is Part of the Learning

Requirements shift, team dynamics evolve, clients face unexpected constraints, and research can reveal surprising results. These moments are opportunities to practice resilience, adaptability, and ethical decision-making.

When the project changes, return to your shared goals and ask what needs to be renegotiated. Treat scope, timelines, and feedback as visible parts of the partnership instead of problems to hide.

Focus Areas for Active Work

  • Fieldwork and research methods: Observe context carefully, document evidence, and turn interviews or observations into actionable insights.
  • Project tracking: Break goals into milestones, assign owners, identify risks, and record decisions.
  • Partner updates: Share progress, open questions, changes in scope, and upcoming decisions before they become surprises.
  • Feedback loops: Gather feedback at useful points, evaluate what you heard, and explain how it affects the work.
  • Ethical engagement: Protect people’s privacy, respect community knowledge, and revisit who benefits from project outcomes.

A Simple Weekly Workflow

  1. Review the project goal, current milestone, and commitments made to the partner.
  2. Check in with teammates about progress, capacity, blockers, and support needed.
  3. Complete or plan the next research, design, analysis, or development tasks.
  4. Share a concise update with the partner: what happened, what changed, what you need, and what comes next.
  5. Record decisions, feedback, risks, and new questions where the whole team can find them.
  6. Reflect on what you are learning about the project, your collaboration, and your own practice.

Resources for Active Projects

  • Project Management Worksheet: Break project goals into milestones and task assignments.
  • Project Scoping Handout and Terms: Revisit the agreed scope when new needs or constraints emerge.
  • Engaged Learning Office: Learn more about ELO support for students, faculty, and partners.
  • Ask ELO for support: Contact the office when your team needs help navigating a project challenge.

Before You Move On

Confirm that your team and partner agree on the current scope, next milestone, responsibilities, and communication plan. Keep notes that will make the eventual handoff and reflection easier.
